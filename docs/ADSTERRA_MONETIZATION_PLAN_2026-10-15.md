# Adsterra monetization plan for kruthika.fun

**Prepared:** 15 October 2026
**Scope:** Improve monetization without harming search visibility, user trust, Core Web Vitals, or traffic quality.

## Executive recommendation

Do not try to increase revenue by adding as many ads as possible. For this site, the safest approach is to improve **qualified organic traffic, viewability, page engagement, and measurement**, then test one placement or format at a time. CPM is an auction outcome; no placement can guarantee a higher rate.

## What can lower CPM or revenue

Adsterra’s publisher guidance identifies several variables that can move CPM/eCPM: traffic quality and engagement, audience geography and seasonality, device and audience fit, viewability, ad frequency, format choice, and technical implementation. Low CPM can coexist with acceptable revenue if page/session volume is healthy, so track **RPM, revenue per session, viewability, bounce/engagement, and Core Web Vitals** alongside CPM.

Avoid purchased bot traffic, incentivized clicks, misleading buttons, accidental-click layouts, and traffic sources that produce one-second visits. They can damage advertiser confidence, user trust, and the account itself.

## 30-day test plan

### Days 1–3: establish a baseline

Record impressions, fill rate, CPM/eCPM, revenue, page RPM, sessions, engaged sessions, device type, country, page template, and ad placement. Add a simple placement naming convention such as `article_top`, `article_mid`, and `article_end`. Do not change all placements before collecting a baseline.

### Days 4–10: fix technical and layout issues

Verify every Adsterra script is copied exactly as provided, served over HTTPS, and not altered by a script optimizer. Confirm that ad containers reserve enough space to reduce layout shift. Test mobile first, because a sticky or oversized unit can dominate a small viewport. Keep ads visually distinguishable from navigation and editorial links.

### Days 11–17: test viewability and format fit

For long-form articles, test one viewable in-content or native placement after the introduction and before the conclusion, while retaining a clear reading path. Keep the header and hero area useful for readers rather than turning it into an ad wall. Compare one format against the current control for at least several days and segment by device and geography.

### Days 18–24: improve the traffic that advertisers value

Invest in original, useful content and internal linking rather than buying cheap traffic. Use Search Console and analytics to identify pages with strong search impressions, engaged sessions, and a clear reader need. Expand the best-performing topic clusters, update stale pages when the information genuinely changes, and build direct/returning readership without forcing repeat ad exposures.

### Days 25–30: choose winners by total value

Keep a change only if it improves revenue or RPM without materially worsening engagement, mobile usability, CLS, INP, complaints, or abandonment. Ask the Adsterra manager for frequency-cap and format guidance based on the site’s actual GEO/device mix. Document the winning configuration and stop tests that create intrusive experiences.

## Recommended site-level rules

1. Use one primary experiment at a time and keep a control page/template.
2. Reserve layout space for ads to protect CLS.
3. Do not place ads where users expect navigation, chat controls, or download buttons.
4. Avoid excessive popups, forced redirects, auto-play audio, and repeated interstitials.
5. Use clear consent and privacy disclosures where required for the audience and technologies used.
6. Measure revenue per session and page RPM, not CPM alone.
7. Review mobile performance after every ad-script change.
8. Keep editorial pages useful even if every ad is blocked; content should stand on its own.

## What not to promise

There is no ethical or reliable trick that guarantees a higher CPM. High-paying geographies cannot be manufactured safely, and keyword stuffing for advertiser terms can reduce search quality. The durable path is relevant organic traffic, strong page experience, viewable but restrained inventory, and disciplined testing.
